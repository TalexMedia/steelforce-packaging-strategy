How Steelforce Packaging could use it

This would employ a similar format, where each episode tells the story of a Steelforce customer, a can maker for example, through a decision that shaped their business and the results that followed. Those decisions could include how to respond to the new EU import quotas or to a change in tinplate tariffs. The value is that buyers at other Steelforce customers can see how their peers dealt with the supply and cost pressures they face and how working with Steelforce helped them succeed.

Why long-form episodic wins over time
One-off videos are not appointment content. A returning series gives buyers at can makers a reason to come back for the next episode.
You already talk about the industry in long interviews. The longer of your two interviews on The Metal Packager runs 40 minutes. That interview covers tariffs and how the steel industry is working to become more sustainable.
You are on an industry panel in December. Sustainable Metal Packaging Europe 2026 lists you on its expert panel about the future of beverage canmaking.

How Steelforce Packaging could use it

The same format could be built around a fictionalized purchasing team working to get the best pricing on steel, or a fictionalized crew at one of your global warehouses. Regardless, the value of a short scripted comedy is that each episode stands alone with fixed characters so the show is evergreen, it can be viewed in any order and can be watched wherever and whenever your audience chooses.

Why short-form drama is worth the bet
Entertainment is value given first. Your blog and interviews already give buyers at can makers market analysis. A short comedy would give them something to enjoy that asks nothing of them.
The show would be about your customers' working week. FloQast made PBC, a comedy about accounting work, for the accountants and finance teams it sells to. A comedy set at a can maker would be made for the people at the companies you sell to.
The world resets every episode. Each episode can start from something your customers deal with, such as a quota limit, a tariff change or a rush order.
03 Lead With Value

The Legendeering Framework

The methodology behind every Talex production, and behind the two concepts above. Five directives, one core principle.

Legendeering is Talex's proprietary strategy. The core principle is straightforward: lead with value, always. The brand earns trust by giving without expectation, and that trust grows over time because people keep choosing to watch.

Five directives drive it:

  • Give Without Expectation. Value delivered without strings. The kind of generosity that builds loyalty money cannot buy.
  • Be Relentlessly Authentic. Say true things. Show real work. Speak like a human being.
  • It's Not About You. It's About Them. The audience has to come first. It's all about their wants and needs and making something they will choose to watch.
  • Start With Story, Follow With Facts. Story gives information shape, context, and emotional weight. Facts alone rarely move people.
  • Consistency Is Key. Trust is built in moments, repeated over time.

Legendeering is not limited to the formats described above. Talex produces video for a variety of clients, across a range of industries in all available formats.
